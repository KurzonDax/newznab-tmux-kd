# Music identity is resolved asynchronously from immutable evidence, never inline in the audio pipeline

The legacy `mus` matcher attached the first iTunes search hit as album identity; a conservative prod audit found at least 23 of its 57 positive links attached to the wrong album, while the `aud` path was discarding its best evidence (full archive listings, NZB filename lists) after sampling one track. We decided to replace this with a MusicBrainz-backed identification module (series map on issue #314): the `aud` path commits an **immutable evidence revision** per release (always on, purely local), and a rebuilt `mus` worker asynchronously resolves revisions against a configured MusicBrainz endpoint, persists a versioned, explained **identification decision** with bounded candidate snapshots, and applies identity only through separate, reversible **projections** (guarded canonical rename, internal search text, `musicinfo` compatibility row).

Deliberate choices a future reader may be tempted to "fix":

- **Not inline in `aud`.** Embedding MusicBrainz calls in the audio pane would be far less code, but it couples NNTP/preview throughput to metadata-service health and loses replay. Evidence capture and resolution stay separate lanes.
- **Immutable revisions and versioned decisions, not updates in place.** A new algorithm version creates a new decision and makes the standing corpus eligible again through the normal pane loop — that version bump *is* the replay/backfill mechanism, satisfying the standing constraint that validation reprocesses existing releases with no new artisan requeue commands. Back-catalog releases get a revision lazily synthesized from stored NZB/`release_files`/`release_audio_tags` data, never a refetch.
- **The resolver is pure and the score is a ranking, not a probability.** `MusicIdentityResolver` never mutates a release; structural gates (validated MBID, unique disc ID, several distinct recordings converging in order) decide acceptance, and no score bypasses them. Identity scope is explicit: recording, release group, or exact edition — a fingerprint or single fuzzy hit can accept at most a recording and can never rename anything.
- **Dormant until an endpoint is configured.** This is a public repo; no deployment silently sends traffic anywhere. Pointing at public musicbrainz.org is supported and auto-enforces its etiquette (1 req/s, identifying User-Agent); a private mirror is just a config value, never hardcoded.
- **Rename is a required projection behind the strictest gate, not an operator enhancement** (PreDB/Trusted/manual names win; compilations use the release artist credit). Search enrichment lives only in internal index documents — the external API/RSS contract is frozen and gains no fields.
- **Music videos (Audio→Video) are out of scope** — only audio-routed releases have audio evidence; name-only matching for videos would recreate the first-hit failure this replaces.

Settled in a grilling session on 2026-08-28 (issues #299–#314). Rejected shapes: a pipeline-embedded synchronous resolver (fastest, but loses multi-track structure and replay) and a full append-only provider-payload ledger (maximum audit, too much machinery).
