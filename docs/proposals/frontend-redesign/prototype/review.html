Overview & scorecard

routes/web.php · 60 non-admin GET routes · 141 non-admin Blade files

The front end is not one design. It is four or five, layered over time: a shared release table that is actually good, five cover pages that each copied and drifted from one another, two hub families (TV and Movies) built by different hands, and account pages that predate the component library. Almost every complaint in the brief traces back to one root cause: the same information is rendered by different markup in each place it appears, so parity, sizing and colour drift independently. The fix is structural, not cosmetic: one release component with three view modes, one toolbar, one control size scale, one modal base.

1 · Ease of use

Users cannot compare releases where they choose

Completion %, grabs, media info and NFO exist in the list table only. Covers, hubs, basket and mobile drop most of them.

2 · Consistency

4 page-header styles, 9 hand-rolled breadcrumbs, 7 pagination styles

Only 3 of 141 files use x-page-header; zero account pages use x-input.

3 · Data parity

release-results.blade.php:150. Cover queries do not even fetch the signal.

4 · Sizing

Four control heights share one toolbar

30 / 34 / 38 / 42 px across inline-search, sort dropdown, x-button and x-input. Dropdown widths hard-coded w-40…w-64.

5 · Redundancy

Nine search inputs, four implementations

Header, mobile, inline-search, /search page, plus per-page forms on movies, music, games, books, series. One component is dead.

6 · Broken views

Recommended direction

  • One release browser. A single <x-release-browser> renders Table, Cards and Covers from the same row shape and the same "facts strip" (completion · password · media info · NFO · preview). Every listing page, hub, basket and search result mounts it. Parity stops being a per-page chore.
  • One toolbar, one search. Scope-aware search in the header (with a category select) replaces the page forms; per-page fields become a filter drawer on the toolbar. Sort and view switch appear on every listing, including covers.
  • One control scale. Inputs, selects, buttons and dropdown triggers all resolve to 36 px (md) or 30 px (sm) with the same radius and padding. Dropdown panels size to content with a min-width.
  • Same skeleton for equivalent pages. Series page and movie page share an entity template; My Shows and My Movies become one Watchlist; profile view/edit become one Account page with sections.

2b · The Covers view, at volume

open point: view controlreplaces movies/index · music/index · games/index · console/index · books/index · series/viewserieslist

The live system has roughly 15,000 movies (600 pages at today's 25 per page) and thousands of series, albums and books. A covers view for that catalogue has one job: let someone scan a lot of artwork fast, then drill into one title without losing their place. So the default is the small cover size: ten across, poster plus title plus one line, at whatever per-page count the user has set (48 by default, up to 100). An entity card carries entity information only: artwork, title, the root's identifying line (year, artist, network, author, platform), a release-count badge on the artwork, and a watchlist heart. Nothing about any individual release appears until you click, because with several releases per title there is no honest way to pick one to summarise. Click opens every release of that title in a full-width row under its grid row, in the List-view table, with keyboard left/right to step to the neighbouring title. The page-size control, sticky toolbar, "go to page" and, for alphabetical roots, an A–Z jump strip make 150 pages navigable where 600 were not. On the two button groups: the first is View and picks what a card is: Table (one release per row), Cards (one release per card, with artwork), Covers (one title per card, its releases on click). The second was unlabelled and always visible, which made it look like a second view control. It is now labelled Cover size and appears only while Covers is active: S (10 across), L (6 across), XL (2 across, an entity card with its metadata and its releases listed, which is today's Movies layout). Table likewise gets its own secondary toggle, thumbnails on/off, only while Table is active. Nothing was removed. Per page is a separate, single setting in the pager (24 / 48 / 100) that applies to every view; cover size no longer changes it.

5 · TV & Movie hubs

/series/{id?} · /trending-tv · /myshows · /movie/{imdbid} · /trending-movies · /mymovies

TV and Movies were built as two products. The series list is an A–Z index with a title/year form; the movie list is a cover grid with filters. The series page has a stat grid, season tabs and bulk actions; the movie page has key/value rows and client-side quality buttons and no bulk actions. My Shows is a table; My Movies is a stack of poster cards. The episode and movie release rows are re-implemented and lose completion, grabs, NFO, media info and report.

What is common, and what the decision actually is

Common to both options, not up for decision here: the browse lists become the Covers view of section 2b (TV root and Movies root, same component). The series page and the movie page share one entity template: hero (poster, title, year, rating, external links, a Watch button), then the release browser in Table view with season tabs for TV and quality chips for movies, with bulk select on both. Trending TV and Trending Movies are the same Covers view with a rank badge. The mock under option A shows that entity page.

How a title gets added, today: only through a link to the add form. For movies that link is the purple film button on a release row (only when the release has a valid IMDb id) and a button on the movie page; you cannot add from the Movies covers page or by title. For series it is a button on the series page and on each row of the A–Z list. The link opens a full-page category form.

How a title gets added, proposed: one Watch button, identical for movies and series, in three places: (1) the movie page and series page hero, next to the title; (2) every cover in Covers view, as the heart on the card and in the expanded-releases row header; (3) a release row or card, as a fifth action after Download / Details / Basket / Report, shown only when the release is matched to a movie or series (this replaces the purple film button). Clicking opens a small category picker, defaulting to your last choice, and saves in place. The merged page adds a search box to find a title by name and add it directly, which today's pages cannot do.

The only decision: where the watchlist lives. A: a dedicated Watchlist page (TV and Movies tabs, one card design) replacing My Shows and My Movies. B: no Watchlist page; the covers views get a "Watching" filter and a heart on every card, and watchlist preferences live in the entity page's Watch button. The mock under option B shows the TV covers view with that filter on. Recommendation: A, and take the heart-on-covers from B as well, since it costs nothing once the card exists.

7 · Shared primitives the options depend on

resources/views/components · resources/css/app.css · scripts/check-design-system.sh

Whichever options are chosen, these five primitives are the prerequisite. They are small, and each one retires a class of drift.

Control scale

Two heights only: md 36 px (h-9 px-3 text-sm rounded-lg) and sm 30 px. x-input, x-select, x-button, sort/filter triggers and the search box all use it. Dropdown panels: min-w-48 w-max max-w-80, never a fixed width.

x-modal

One base: backdrop, panel on .card, header/body/footer slots, close button, focus trap, scroll lock, Esc and backdrop click. NFO, files, media info, preview, image, report and confirm become 10-line wrappers.

x-page-header + x-breadcrumb everywhere

Title, optional subtitle, actions slot, optional tabs slot. Replaces the four hub headers, nine breadcrumbs, and the hero gradients.

Lint what the rules say

Extend check-design-system.sh to fail on bg-white/bg-gray-* surfaces outside components, indigo-*/purple-* accents, FA4 icon names, and blue-* inside resources/js. Today it checks two of the eight documented rules.

Chip colour semantics, fixed once

Completion good/warn/bad = green/yellow/red. Password = red. Media info, NFO, preview, group = neutral or primary. "View Series" and "My Movies" lose their indigo/purple. Reported = orange stays.
